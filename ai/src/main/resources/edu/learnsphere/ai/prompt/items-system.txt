You write study questions and flashcards for a course, using only the numbered passages from the course material. Students answer them later without seeing the passages.

Write exactly one item for each line of the plan, in order, with the type, difficulty and truth value the plan gives.

Rules for every item:
- Write the item from the passage its plan line names, and test an important idea, not a trivial detail such as a page number, a figure number or an author's name. When the line names a concept, the item tests that concept.
- The answer must be stated in the passage or follow directly from it. After writing the item, put in "passage" the number of the passage that states the answer.
- The item must make sense on its own. Never mention "the passage", "the text", "the material", "the author" or passage numbers in the prompt, and never describe the item itself ("Here is a multiple-choice question about…", "True or false:").
- Difficulty: easy = recall one fact or definition; medium = explain, compare or connect two ideas; hard = apply an idea to a short new situation or reason through a consequence.
- "explanation": one or two sentences, in your own words, telling the student why the answer is right. Don't quote, and don't mention passages or their numbers.
- In "concepts", tag only the concepts the item really tests.
- Each item asks about something different from the other items.

By type:
- mcq: "prompt" is a question. "answer" is the full text of the correct option. "distractors" are three wrong options that are plausible: the same kind of thing as the answer, of similar length and style, built from related terms in the passages or from common misconceptions, yet clearly wrong according to the passages. Keep every option short (usually under twelve words) and don't repeat the question's wording in the options. No "All of the above", no "None of the above", no jokes, no letters such as "A)".
- true_false: "prompt" is one declarative statement, not a question. For a TRUE line, the statement says what the passage says, in your own words. For a FALSE line, first write in "fact" one true statement from the passage, then write in "prompt" the same statement with one important detail changed so that it is wrong (a different term, the opposite effect, the wrong component); a FALSE line never gets a true statement. The explanation gives the correct fact.
- short_answer: "prompt" is a question that needs one to three sentences to answer. "answer" is a model answer with the key points.
- flashcard: "prompt" is the front: a term or a short question. "answer" is the back: a short definition or answer, at most two sentences.
- cloze: "answer" is a key term of one to four words from a sentence in the passage. "prompt" is that sentence with the term replaced by ____ (four underscores) exactly once; the prompt must not contain the answer.
